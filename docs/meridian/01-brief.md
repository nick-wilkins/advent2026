# Brief

## Background

Last year each child had an individual daily challenge that revealed a three-digit padlock code. Both padlocks secured a shared box holding portions of a LEGO set. The children loved the physical challenges (string trails, retrieving objects, opening the box). Designing two puzzles every day was too time-consuming.

This year: a more ambitious ongoing mystery (expeditions, secret agents, science, artefacts, hidden messages, an eccentric professor) with much less daily preparation.

## The experience

It should feel like a real adventure that has come to find the children, not an Advent calendar Dad made. Ingredients: Indiana Jones exploration, secret agents and detective work, escape rooms, international travel, ancient artefacts, practical science and engineering, physical challenges, treasure hunting, genuine revelations, a little humour and possibly a little magic. The children should speculate, revisit earlier discoveries and look forward to what comes next. It builds to a Christmas Eve finale.

## Non-negotiable principles

### Fairness
- Both children have equivalent experiences every day, ideally the same type of activity. Never one child on a physical adventure while the other does a worksheet.
- Allowed differences: answers, clues, artefacts, equipment colour, slightly different difficulty, complementary halves of a shared discovery.
- Neither child is the protagonist. No competitive scoring. Collaboration matters.
- Identities: **Dylan (8) is Agent Blue**, **Flynn (6) is Agent Orange**. Nick had no preference; swap freely before printing.
- **Age gap:** Flynn is two years younger. Same activity type every day, but Orange materials use fewer words, larger print, more pictures and simpler number work. Neither child should notice the difference. Never let the gap turn Dylan into the reader for both.

### Physical first
- Prioritise physical objects, letters, tangible clues, adventures around the house, building, real tools, investigating evidence, hidden objects and experiments.
- Paper puzzles are fine but must not dominate.
- Technology enhances the illusion; it is never the adventure.

### Low daily parental effort
- About 70% of days need under five minutes of setup. Some need nothing beyond putting out an envelope. A handful of bigger physical set pieces. A substantial Christmas Eve finale.
- Everything is prepared upfront in numbered daily envelopes: instructions, printables, clues, solutions and setup notes.
- Reuse mechanics. We do not need 48 unique puzzles.

### Meaningful progression
- Something tangible every day: artefact, evidence, equipment, puzzle piece, banknote or story discovery.
- Early objects become useful later. Favourite mechanic: an ordinary or mysterious object whose true purpose is revealed much later.
- The children accumulate personal equipment, expedition records, shared artefacts, evidence, machine components and knowledge.

### Story first
- Develop the story, then use objects that support it. Work backwards from Christmas Eve.
- Exciting and mysterious, never genuinely frightening. The children and family are never in real danger.

## Delivery model

- A large woven basket is the Order's **supply drop**. Most mornings it holds some mix of: airmail envelope, shared story letter, blue and orange mission envelopes, evidence bag, artefact, locked container, or an incoming call.
- **Padlock days:** each child finds their own three-digit code; both codes open a shared box. Aim for 14 to 16 padlock days, not every day.
- Some events can happen at dinner time (surprise deliveries, audio messages).
- Each child has an A5 **field journal**: banknote sleeves, stamps, photographs, symbols, evidence, notes, certificates. Optional keepsake, never homework.
- **Outbox** (proposed in the story): reply slips left in the basket overnight; the next letter responds to them.

## Provisional chapter structure

- **I Recruitment (1 to 6):** meet the Professor, become agents, journals and world book, detective kit, something has happened, first milestone.
- **II Following the Professor (7 to 12):** international route, banknotes, equipment, start the Engine, suspicious evidence, first big twist.
- **III Uncovering the truth (13 to 18):** old equipment reused, hidden messages, wooden jigsaws (not before 12 Dec), hollow book, major truth.
- **IV Recovering the Stone (19 to 24):** combine evidence, purpose of the machine, dragon Guardian, final location, Christmas Eve hunt (30 to 45 minutes), congratulations and certificates. Both children have equally important roles.
