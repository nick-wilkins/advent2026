# Story (Stage 2, adopted)

## 1. Three directions

### A. The Fiftieth Year (recommended)

**Premise.** The Meridian Stone has not been stolen. Rowan Ashwood took it. She was the Professor's oldest friend and his fellow Keeper. The Order has one rule: the Stone is always kept by two people, and every fifty years it passes to two new young Keepers. The last handover happened in 1976, when Ashwood was eight and Thistlewick was six, the same ages as Dylan and Flynn. This year is the fiftieth. The Professor, being the Professor, has forgotten. He and Rowan have not spoken for twenty years, so nobody reminded him.

Rowan never forgets anything. She took the Stone, laid a trail along the route of their first expedition and built every clue for the two children she had chosen. Then she made sure their names turned up in the Professor's files. He believes he is chasing a thief. In fact he is being guided, together with the children, to the handover.

**Strengths**
- One idea answers almost every open question from the handover: why two children, why the "antagonist" is really a protector, why the Stone is at home, why the clues suit a 6 and an 8 year old (Rowan designed them for these two), why the banknotes matter, and why it ends on Christmas Eve.
- The Professor's running jokes drive the plot. His forgetfulness caused the whole mystery, and the falling-out with Rowan started over the last biscuit.
- The emotional thread is one children understand: two friends who fell out, mended by two siblings who have to cooperate. You don't need to state the parallel; they will feel it.
- It ends with the children *becoming* something (Keepers, 2026 to 2076), not just collecting a prize. That gives you a natural hook for next year.
- There is no villain and no danger. The stakes are emotional: the Order ends unless the handover happens.

**Weaknesses and how to handle them**
- The friendship backstory has to land with a seven-year-old. Tell it through objects, not exposition: a torn photograph, the Professor's old mortar, a biscuit tin. Keep each piece of backstory to a sentence or two.
- The children may guess Rowan is friendly early on. That's fine. Being ahead of the grown-up is one of the best feelings this age group can have. The Professor's stubborn denial ("R.A.? Could be anyone. Reginald Applegate. Ruth Abernathy.") turns their early guess into a joke they are in on.

### B. The Saboteur

A classic whodunit. Someone inside the Order sabotaged the Engine and took the Stone. The children collect evidence and witness statements about three suspects (Rowan, Magnus Cogwell and the Professor's assistant), then name the culprit on Christmas Eve.

- **Strengths:** makes full use of the detective kit, gives every day an obvious job (gather evidence) and builds to a clear deduction.
- **Weaknesses:** it relies on paper. Suspect profiles and contradictory statements are worksheets. Holding three suspects in mind for three weeks is a lot for a seven-year-old, and deduction favours the older child, which breaks your fairness rule. It needs a real wrongdoer, which works against "the antagonist was protecting it". The Stone being at home has no reason. Crime stories also edge towards "someone took something from a house", which can unsettle young children.

### C. The Missing Professor

The Professor disappears partway through an expedition. The children follow his trail of banknotes and photographs around the world to bring him home. The Stone is the way he finds his way back.

- **Strengths:** real urgency, travel at the centre of every day, and a strong reason to follow the route.
- **Weaknesses:** a missing adult is genuine peril, which you have ruled out. It also takes the Professor off the phone, and he is your best device. The ending is a rescue, not a discovery, so the house twist turns into "he was hiding at home", which feels like a cheat.

**Rejected variant: "the Professor hid it and forgot."** It's funny once, but it makes the whole month his mistake and leaves no second character to care about. Direction A keeps the joke on a smaller scale: he forgot the *date*, not the hiding place.

## 2. The recommended story in detail

### Logline

A forgetful old explorer recruits two children to recover a stolen crystal. They gradually discover that the "thief" is his long-lost best friend, that the trail was made for them, and that the Stone was never lost at all. It was waiting for them at home.

### The Order's legend (what the children learn by the end)

- The **Meridian Stone** is a pink crystal brought home by the Order's first explorers long ago. Explorers say that if you are lost, its light helps you find your way home.
- The Order's name comes from its oldest legend: the **Lost Meridian**, an invisible line on the map that leads every traveller home. Wherever the Stone is kept, that's where the line runs.
- **The Rule of Two:** "Two must travel. Two must find. Two must keep." The Stone has always had two Keepers. A single Keeper can't hold it for long; it slowly goes dark.
- Every fifty years, two new young Keepers are chosen and the Stone is handed on.
- The motto, written on every letterhead from Day 1: ***Omnis via domum ducit*** ("Every road leads home"). The children won't understand it until the end. Translate it for them on Christmas Eve.
- The emblem: a small dragon curled around a vertical meridian line. It's on every document from Day 1, and it is the Guardian they build in Chapter IV.

Keep the magic ambiguous. Nobody says outright that the Stone is magic. It glows when they find it (a hidden LED; see the finale), and the children can decide what that means.

### The secret history (parent's truth, in order)

1. **1976:** Two children, Agent Red (young Thistlewick) and Agent Green (young Rowan Ashwood), are made Keepers by the old Keepers. They spend the next ten years on expeditions together, collecting a banknote from every country they visit for the Order's **Atlas of Notes**.
2. **About 2006:** They argue over something so small the Professor can't remember what it was (the last biscuit in the tin). Rowan leaves. The Professor keeps the Stone alone in his study for twenty years. It has grown dim, and he hasn't noticed.
3. **Autumn 2026:** Rowan realises it's the handover year and the Professor has forgotten. She chooses two new Keepers (your children), slips their names into the Order's records of "promising young explorers", sends the Stone ahead to their home, and takes Rowan's half of the Atlas of Notes and the dismantled Meridian Engine to lay the trail.
4. **Late November:** The Professor finds the Stone's case empty, with a note: *"Only two can follow."* He searches the records for two explorers and finds your children's names. He's not entirely sure how they got there.
5. **December:** The children follow the trail. The Professor travels along it at the same time, always one step behind *them*. The clues only open for the children, so he depends on their answers.
6. **24 December:** The children find the Stone at home, become Keepers, and the Professor and Rowan finally talk again.

### What the children believe at each stage

| Days | They believe | What's actually true | Turning point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 6 | A thief took the Professor's Stone and only we can follow the trail | Rowan is guiding the trail | **Day 6:** the thief *wants* to be followed |
| 7 to 12 | Someone called "R.A." is leading the Professor around the world | R.A. is his oldest friend | **Day 12:** the torn photograph names Rowan |
| 13 to 18 | Rowan stole it. Is she a baddie? | Rowan is protecting it and handing it on | **Day 17:** the hollow book |
| 19 to 23 | We have to find where Rowan hid the Stone | It's in our house | **Day 21:** the jigsaw map is our house |
| 24 | Find the Stone | We are the new Keepers | Christmas Eve |

### The revelations

1. **"The thief wants to be followed" (Day 6, end of Chapter I).** Evidence from the Professor's study (photographs, a fingerprint card, the note) shows that nothing was forced and the thief left a careful trail on purpose. Then comes the first intercepted message, hidden in red visual noise and signed only **R.A.**: *"Good. You found the first door. Keep going, both of you."* The question every child will ask: why would a thief help us?

2. **"R.A. is Rowan Ashwood" (Day 12, end of Chapter II).** Each child has been carrying half of a torn black-and-white photograph since early in the month. Put together, it shows two young explorers in the 1980s, labelled on the back *"T. & R.A., Kathmandu, 1984"* (or whichever country you choose from the banknotes). The Professor finally admits who she is, and his voice cracks a little. This is the moment the children start caring about him rather than just the puzzles.

3. **"Rowan isn't stealing it, she's giving it away" (Day 17, Chapter III).** The thick book that arrived on Day 3 and has sat unopened for two weeks has a hidden compartment. Inside is Rowan's letter to the children, the long old key and a photograph of an empty, glowing space. She explains (briefly, warmly) that this is the fiftieth year, the Professor forgot, and she chose them. The book was in their house the whole time, which quietly sets up revelation 4.

4. **"The Stone is here" (Day 21, Chapter IV).** The two wooden jigsaws the children have been assembling since 13 December are not a foreign map. Put together, they show a hand-drawn plan of **their own house**. The banknote route on the world map loops back to the UK; the last note in the Atlas is a British one. "Every road leads home."

5. **"You are the Keepers" (24 December).** Inside the final container are the glowing Stone and a second old photograph: two children, aged eight and six, holding the same Stone in 1976 (just like Dylan and Flynn). The back says *"Agent Red and Agent Green, Keepers 1976 to 2026."* Then you take the 2026 photograph of your two holding it. That's the moment they'll remember.

### Characters

See `04-characters.md`. Two speaking characters only: Professor Thistlewick and Rowan Ashwood. Magnus Cogwell is cut.

### Why the children are needed (stated in story terms)

- The Rule of Two: two must travel, two must find. Rowan built every clue to need two people, and the Professor has tried and failed to open them alone.
- Their names were in the records. (Rowan put them there; revealed on Day 17.)
- The clues only make sense to them. They are addressed to Agent Blue and Agent Orange and use objects in their basket.
- The Professor is following the same trail and literally waits for their answers. **The Outbox:** a reply slip goes back in the basket overnight, and the next letter refers to what they wrote. It takes almost no parental effort, keeps things off screens and is very powerful: "He read our note!"

### Objects, Engine and banknotes

See `05-objects-and-mechanics.md`.

### Chapter beats

**Chapter I: Recruitment (1 to 6).** The call, the basket, agent identities, the Field Kit and the Professor's first photographs (his study, the empty case, the Stone on his desk in better days and the mortar in the background). The hollow book arrives on Day 3 without explanation; the Professor says, "No idea who sent that. Put it somewhere safe." Hold one shared investigation of the study evidence. **Ends:** the thief wants to be followed; first note from R.A.

**Chapter II: Following the trail (7 to 12).** The international route begins: banknotes, stamps and calls from abroad. The first Engine batches arrive and each child starts their tower. The Court Figures turn up. The mortar arrives and the Professor is astonished. R.A.'s notes keep helping, and the Professor keeps refusing to think about who it might be. **Ends:** the torn photograph is completed and names Rowan.

**Chapter III: The truth (13 to 18).** The jigsaw pieces begin to arrive, presented as "Rowan's map". Earlier equipment comes back with new uses (lenses, mirrors, figures). Hold the puzzle-egg day. The Professor slowly tells their story, one small memory per call, and admits he can't remember why they fell out. **Day 17:** the hollow book. **Day 18:** Rowan's first voice message, kind and a bit dry.

**Chapter IV: Coming home (19 to 24).** The Engine is completed (Day 20). The jigsaw map is revealed as their house (Day 21). The Guardian is built (Day 22 or 23). The Professor finally calls Rowan off-stage. The children don't hear the call; they hear about it the next day ("We talked for three hours. I'd forgotten how much she laughs."). **Day 24:** the finale.

### Christmas Eve finale (about 40 minutes, late afternoon)

1. **Final briefing call.** The Professor, nervous and excited: "Tonight's the night it's always been. Fifty years ago tonight, I was six."
2. **Two trails at once.** Blue wool and orange wool, each starting at the basket and winding to a separate part of the house. Each trail collects that child's final pieces: a last red-lens message, a last mirror message and the final digit card. Both trails have the same length and the same kinds of stops.
3. **The meeting point.** The trails end at the same place, for example under the Christmas tree. Messages only make sense together: Blue's red-lens text gives the room, Orange's mirror text gives the place in that room.
4. **The Keeper's Key and the Guardian.** The Guardian (dragon) is placed on the jigsaw map at the marked spot, and the symbols under the Court Figures confirm it. The long key opens nothing literally. Its silhouette matches a drawing on the jigsaw that shows exactly where to look.
5. **The Stone.** A container that has been hidden since 30 November inside a blanket-wrapped box under Nick's bed (see the hiding place note below), unknown to the children. It is locked with both padlocks; each child opens their own with their final code. Inside, the Stone rests on a small battery LED puck, already switched on, so it glows in the dark box. The 1976 photograph is underneath. The Stone has to be lifted out by both children together (the Rule of Two).
6. **The final call, two voices.** The Professor and Rowan together, bickering fondly about who gets the last biscuit. The Professor finds his spectacles on his head. They name your children as Keepers, 2026 to 2076, and translate the motto: "Every road leads home."
7. **Certificates and the photograph.** Keeper certificates, then you take the 2026 photograph matching the 1976 one. Print it and put it in the field journals.

**How did the Stone get into the house without a stranger coming in?** Rowan *posted* it in late November in a sealed parcel addressed to Dad, the Order's **Quartermaster**, with instructions to hide it without opening it. This is revealed in the final call: "And thank you to your Quartermaster for keeping it safe." It explains everything practical (deliveries, timing, why Dad is always around) and turns the boys' suspicion of Dad into part of the story rather than something that breaks it. **Decided:** Nick is the Quartermaster.

**Hiding place (decided):** under Nick's bed, in a lidded box wrapped in a blanket, pushed to the middle against the wall. This fits the story (the Quartermaster hid it), the boys rarely go there, and it doesn't favour either boy's bed. The toilet cistern was rejected: water and electronics (the LED puck) don't mix, a heavy ceramic lid could crush fingers, and the boys would be lifting it at bedtime on Christmas Eve. Switch the LED on and check the battery during setup on the 24th.

### Fairness built into the story

The Rule of Two makes fairness part of the story itself:
- Every object comes in pairs or halves: two lenses, two mirrors, two photo halves, two towers, two jigsaws, two padlocks, two trails.
- Every revelation needs both children's halves. No child ever holds the full answer alone.
- Rowan's notes are always addressed to both of them. The Professor alternates who he speaks to first on calls.
- One-off shared items (the skull egg, the detective hat, the LEGO build) use explicit turn structures: alternate instruction pages, or one holds while the other steers, then swap halfway.

## 3. Sample voice

### Revised opening call (Day 1)

```
Hello? Hello? Is this thing working?

Oh, marvellous. Right. I haven't got long.

Dylan. Flynn. My name is Professor Thistlewick, of the Order of the Lost Meridian. You won't have heard of us. Hardly anybody has. That's rather the point.

Two nights ago, the most important object the Order has ever kept vanished from my study. The Meridian Stone. Nothing broken. Nothing out of place. Just an empty case, and a note in its place. Four words.

"Only two can follow."

Now. I have been an explorer for a very long time. Six continents. Forty years. One extremely rude camel called Humphrey. And I have never met a clue I couldn't follow myself.

But this one won't open for me. I've tried. Twice. Possibly three times.

So I searched the Order's records for two explorers. Two people who might just work well together. And there were your names. I'm not entirely sure how they got there. But there they were.

I've sent you a basket. Keep everything in it. Even the dull-looking things. Especially the dull-looking things.

From now on, Dylan, you are Agent Blue. Flynn, you are Agent Orange. Neither of you is in charge. Neither of you can do this alone. That's the rule.

Open your envelopes. I'll be in touch.

And agents? If anybody asks, you've never heard of me.

Thistlewick out.

...Now which button... no, that's the kettle...
```

### Rowan's first note (Day 6, hidden under red noise)

```
Good. You found the first door.
He'll say I'm a thief. Let him.
Keep going, both of you. Together.
R.A.
```

## 4. Ideas challenged or changed

- **The stolen-magical-object frame** is kept on the surface but turned inside out: the "theft" is a gift, and the real mystery is the Order.
- **The Stone "brought back by the Professor and hidden at home"** didn't explain why it would be at *their* home. Now Rowan sends it to the Keepers she has chosen.
- **The hollow book can't hold the Stone.** A 60mm crystal won't fit a believable cavity. The book holds the letter and the key, which is the better revelation anyway.
- **The marble run as the final mechanism** is kept as a ceremony and a contributor, with a static backup clue, as above.
- **Magnus Cogwell** is cut as a character.
- **Intercepted messages from an unknown sender** now come from a single sender, R.A. Two mysterious voices would be one too many.
- **The LEGO dragon** becomes one shared build with a story identity from Day 1 (the emblem), not a daily reward.
- **The hydrometer as a "Meridian Density Detector"** is too grand for one experiment. It's just the Professor's float glass, used once.
- **The detective hat:** whoever's turn it is to lead an investigation wears it, and turns alternate. Or it stays on the basket as the Professor's spare.
- **The Astral Compass** is dropped. The Engine already gives the shared construction element.
